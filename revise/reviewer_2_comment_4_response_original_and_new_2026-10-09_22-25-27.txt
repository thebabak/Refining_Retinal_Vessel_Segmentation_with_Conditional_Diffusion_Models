Response to Reviewer 2, Comment 4

Thank you for this comment. We agree that the original results alone do not isolate the contributions of individual components. We have now conducted a separate controlled component-ablation study on CHASE-DB1, DRIVE, and HRF, with five independently trained refiners per training configuration. This study includes a deterministic learned refiner, target diffusion, removal of image conditioning, spatial instead of global conditioning, additive versus FiLM conditioning injection, and frozen versus jointly fine-tuned mask autoencoders.

Within the new study, each comparison uses the same dataset split and matched frozen LU-Net+RA and initial autoencoder checkpoints for each training seed. The deterministic control is a latent residual refiner using the corresponding denoising-network architecture, rather than the earlier RGB-plus-coarse spatial U-Net. Target diffusion was evaluated with both coarse-map initialization and pure Gaussian initialization; the latter also changes the inference initialization and is therefore not a single-factor comparison. The autoencoder fine-tuning condition updates additional parameters. Numerical results below are mean Dice, rounded to six decimal places; the accompanying source table retains full precision and between-seed standard deviations.

The first two rows preserve the original author-supplied results and are provided separately as context. They are not the matched reference for the newly executed ablations: the available executable implementation and checkpoints did not reproduce the original diffusion means. We therefore compare the new ablations with the new global-conditioning/frozen-autoencoder reference, and do not use the original diffusion row to infer a controlled advantage over the new refiners.

The new results do not uniformly support the original design choices. The deterministic refiner and joint autoencoder fine-tuning yield higher mean Dice than the new reference on all three datasets. Changes to image conditioning and conditioning injection produce small mean differences; these differences were not significant under the Holm-adjusted paired training-seed t-tests. Target diffusion with coarse initialization has mixed results, while pure Gaussian initialization performs poorly in the tested configuration. These observations do not establish that the reference design is optimal or that diffusion is superior to deterministic refinement.

The study provides the requested component comparisons for the newly executed configuration, while leaving the original author-supplied results unchanged. Its conclusions are limited to that configuration and the small fixed test cohorts. This response and the consolidated results have been saved separately; the new component-ablation study has not yet been incorporated into the manuscript.
