Thank you for this comment. We conducted a new controlled component-ablation study on CHASE-DB1, DRIVE, and HRF. For each dataset we used five matched training-seed settings, retaining exactly the same per-seed coarse-predictor checkpoint, initial autoencoder, data split, coarse probability maps, and evaluation protocol. Seven configurations were trained separately for 100 epochs each, producing 105 trained refiners. Five matched sampling realisations were evaluated at 50 DDIM transitions for stochastic methods; the deterministic model was evaluated once per image. All 7,200 image/training-seed/sampling records were verified against saved FP32 predictions, ground truth, FOV masks, and confusion counts.

The study includes the four requested controls—deterministic refinement, target diffusion, no image conditioning, and spatial rather than global conditioning—and additionally compares additive versus FiLM injection and frozen versus jointly fine-tuned autoencoder weights. Target diffusion is evaluated with both coarse warm-start and full-Gaussian initialisation, using the same target-trained checkpoint. The warm-start comparison changes the diffused training target; full-Gaussian inference also changes initialisation and is therefore not a single-factor comparison.

We report paired training-seed t tests, exact signed-rank tests, separate Holm adjustment across 24 comparisons for each test family, and exploratory crossed-bootstrap 95% intervals with 50,000 resamples. CHASE resampling retains both eyes within each of four subjects; DRIVE uses image-level resampling, and HRF uses pathology-stratified image resampling. Sampling repetitions are averaged before calculating between-training-seed SD; they are not counted as independent training runs.

Global FiLM, spatial FiLM, target diffusion, additive injection, and the matched latent deterministic control each update 405,824 parameters in this executable configuration. No-image conditioning updates 378,112; jointly fine-tuning the autoencoder updates 527,233. The additive implementation combines the existing two affine projections, matching stored parameter count but using a redundant additive parameterisation. Equal parameter budgets do not imply equal effective capacity, activation memory, or computational cost.

Within this new implementation, the matched latent deterministic refiner had higher mean Dice than the frozen/global diffusion reference on all three datasets. Joint autoencoder fine-tuning also increased mean Dice. Changes from removing RGB conditioning, using spatial conditioning, or switching to additive injection were small, with no Holm-adjusted paired t-test significance for these conditioning comparisons. Target warm-start effects were mixed across datasets, and full-Gaussian target initialisation performed poorly in this globally conditioned configuration. None of the exact signed-rank comparisons remained significant after Holm adjustment; with only five pairs, these tests have coarse resolution. The parametric and nonparametric evidence should therefore be reported together. These results do not demonstrate that the original selected design is optimal.

These experiments quantify component effects within the retained executable configuration. They are separate from the original author-supplied positive-score table and do not reproduce or validate its score-producing checkpoints or architectural parameter budget. The design is not claimed to be universally optimal, and the small fixed test sets and five training pairs limit inference. Full statistical results and per-image records accompany this response.
