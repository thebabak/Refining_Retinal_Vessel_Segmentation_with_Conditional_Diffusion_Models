Suggested reviewer response:
Thank you for this comment. Examination of the executable rerun code establishes the protocol used for our separate new experiments. CHASE-DB1 is divided by subject into 16 training, 4 validation and 8 test images (8, 2 and 4 subjects), retaining both eyes of each subject in one partition. DRIVE uses its official 20-image test set, with 16 training and 4 validation images drawn from the training pool. HRF uses a pathology-stratified 27/6/12 training/validation/test split, with 9/2/4 images from each pathology category. Thus, the 20 and 33 non-test images include validation images in these new experiments.
Predictions are binarised at probability >=0.5. Metrics are computed per image and averaged rather than calculated from pooled pixels. CHASE-DB1 is evaluated over the whole image; DRIVE uses the local dataset FOV GIF masks, and HRF uses the field-of-view bitmap in the local annotation JSON. Binary masks are resized using nearest-neighbour interpolation. The upstream conversion provenance of the HRF annotation files remains undocumented.
The coarse predictor is selected by validation mean Dice, with a maximum of 1000 epochs and early-stopping patience of 20. The newly trained component-ablation refiners use the predefined final checkpoint at epoch100, without test-based selection. These settings document the newly executed experiments. We cannot establish from these scripts alone that the original author-supplied diffusion means were produced using the same protocol, so we do not retrospectively assign these settings to the original results.
